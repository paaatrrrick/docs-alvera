Neptune is the eighth planet from the Sun and the farthest known planet in our solar system. Like Uranus, it's an ice giant: a cold, deep-blue world that astronomers found through math before anyone saw it through a telescope.

## Discovery

In the early 1800s, astronomers noticed that Uranus wasn't following its predicted path. Urbain Le Verrier in France and John Couch Adams in England worked separately and both concluded that the gravity of an unseen planet was pulling on Uranus. Using Le Verrier's predictions, Johann Galle spotted Neptune from the Berlin Observatory on September 23, 1846. It was very close to where the calculations said it would be.

## Composition and appearance

Neptune's outer atmosphere is mostly hydrogen and helium, with a small amount of methane. Methane absorbs red light and reflects blue light, which gives the planet its blue color. Below the atmosphere is a dense, hot mantle made of water, ammonia, and methane ices, and at the center is a rocky core about the size of Earth.

## Weather

Even though Neptune is so far from the Sun, it has the fastest winds in the solar system, which can reach more than 2,000 km/h. In 1989, Voyager 2 photographed the Great Dark Spot, a huge storm roughly the size of Earth. When the Hubble Space Telescope looked a few years later, that storm was gone, but other dark spots have formed since then.

## Moons and rings

Neptune has 16 known moons. The largest, Triton, orbits in the opposite direction to Neptune's spin, which is called a retrograde orbit. This suggests that Triton is a Kuiper Belt object that Neptune's gravity captured. Triton is also one of the few places in the solar system with active geysers, which shoot nitrogen gas and dark dust high above its icy surface.

Neptune also has a set of faint, dark rings. The outermost ring, called the Adams ring, has unusual bright clumps known as ring arcs.

## Exploration

Only one spacecraft has ever visited Neptune: Voyager 2, which flew past in August 1989. It sent back the first close-up images of the planet, its rings, and Triton. Since then, astronomers have kept studying Neptune with telescopes such as Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). In 2022, JWST captured a clear view of Neptune's rings.
