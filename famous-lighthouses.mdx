## Overview

Lighthouses have guided ships safely to shore for thousands of years, standing at the boundary between land and sea. This page highlights some of the most famous lighthouses in history, from ancient wonders to modern engineering marvels.

## Ancient lighthouses

### Lighthouse of Alexandria

Built on the island of Pharos in Egypt around 280 BCE, the Lighthouse of Alexandria was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. Estimates put its height between 100 and 130 meters, making it one of the tallest structures on Earth for centuries. Earthquakes damaged it repeatedly, and it finally collapsed in 1323.

### Tower of Hercules

Located in A Coruña, Spain, the Tower of Hercules is the oldest known Roman lighthouse still in use today. Built in the 2nd century CE, it remains an active aid to navigation and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Iconic European lighthouses

### Eddystone Lighthouse

Sited on the treacherous Eddystone Rocks off the coast of Cornwall, England, the Eddystone Lighthouse has been rebuilt four times since 1698. Each iteration advanced lighthouse engineering, culminating in James Douglass's 1882 tower, which still stands today.

### Bell Rock Lighthouse

Off the east coast of Scotland, Bell Rock Lighthouse has operated continuously since 1811. Engineered by Robert Stevenson, it is the world's oldest surviving sea-washed lighthouse and remains a benchmark of maritime construction.

### Fastnet Lighthouse

Perched on a rocky outcrop off the southwest coast of Ireland, Fastnet is the tallest and widest rock lighthouse in Ireland and the United Kingdom. Sailors know it as the turning point of the famous Fastnet Race.

## Iconic North American lighthouses

### Boston Light

Boston Light, on Little Brewster Island in Massachusetts, was the first lighthouse built in what would become the United States. Established in 1716, it is the last officially staffed lighthouse in the country.

### Cape Hatteras Lighthouse

Standing 60 meters tall on the Outer Banks of North Carolina, Cape Hatteras Lighthouse is the tallest brick lighthouse in the United States. Its distinctive black-and-white spiral stripes guide mariners past the notoriously dangerous Diamond Shoals.

### Peggy's Point Lighthouse

Also known simply as Peggy's Cove Lighthouse, this small octagonal tower in Nova Scotia has become one of the most photographed lighthouses in the world, thanks to the dramatic granite coastline surrounding it.

## Iconic lighthouses around the world

### La Jument

Off the coast of Brittany, France, La Jument is famous less for its engineering than for a 1989 photograph by Jean Guichard capturing keeper Théodore Malgorn standing in the doorway as a massive wave engulfs the tower.

### Cape Point Lighthouse

Overlooking the meeting of the Atlantic and Indian Oceans near the southern tip of Africa, Cape Point Lighthouse has warned ships away from the treacherous waters off South Africa's Cape Peninsula since 1860.

### Macquarie Lighthouse

Australia's first and longest-operating lighthouse, Macquarie Lighthouse has marked the entrance to Sydney Harbour since 1818. The current sandstone tower dates from 1883.

## Anatomy of a lighthouse

Most traditional lighthouses share a common set of components:

- **Tower** — the vertical structure that raises the light above sea level so it can be seen from a distance.
- **Lantern room** — the glass-enclosed space at the top that houses the light source.
- **Fresnel lens** — a compact, powerful lens invented in 1822 that revolutionized lighthouse optics.
- **Foghorn** — an audible warning used when visibility is poor.
- **Keeper's quarters** — living space for the lighthouse keeper, though most lighthouses are now automated.

## Why lighthouses still matter

Even in an era of GPS and electronic charts, lighthouses remain valuable as backup navigation aids, cultural landmarks, and symbols of maritime heritage. Many decommissioned lighthouses now serve as museums, inns, or protected historic sites, keeping their stories alive for new generations.
