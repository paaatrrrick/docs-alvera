## Overview

Lighthouses are towers that emit light to guide ships safely along coastlines, past hazards, and into harbors. For thousands of years, they have combined engineering, architecture, and maritime tradition. This page highlights some of the most famous lighthouses in the world.

## Ancient lighthouses

### Lighthouse of Alexandria (Pharos)

- **Location:** Alexandria, Egypt
- **Built:** ~280 BCE, during the reign of Ptolemy II
- **Height:** Estimated 100–130 meters

One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, the Pharos of Alexandria stood on the island of Pharos and guided sailors into the harbor with a mirror that reflected sunlight by day and fire by night. It was damaged by earthquakes and finally collapsed in the 14th century.

### Tower of Hercules

- **Location:** A Coruña, Spain
- **Built:** Late 1st or early 2nd century CE (Roman)
- **Height:** 55 meters

The oldest Roman lighthouse still in use today. It was restored in the 18th century and designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2009.

## Iconic European lighthouses

### Eddystone Lighthouse

- **Location:** Eddystone Rocks, English Channel, UK
- **Current tower built:** 1882 (fourth iteration)

The Eddystone Lighthouse is famous for the series of towers built on this treacherous reef, including Smeaton's Tower (1759), which pioneered the interlocking stone design used in modern rock lighthouses.

### Bell Rock Lighthouse

- **Location:** Bell Rock, North Sea, Scotland
- **Built:** 1810
- **Height:** 35 meters

Designed by Robert Stevenson, Bell Rock is the world's oldest surviving sea-washed lighthouse and continues to operate today.

### Fastnet Lighthouse

- **Location:** Fastnet Rock, off County Cork, Ireland
- **Built:** 1904 (current tower)

Known as "Ireland's Teardrop" because it was the last sight of Ireland for emigrants sailing to America.

## North American lighthouses

### Boston Light

- **Location:** Little Brewster Island, Massachusetts, USA
- **First lit:** 1716; rebuilt 1783

The oldest lighthouse station in the United States and the last remaining staffed lighthouse in the country.

### Cape Hatteras Lighthouse

- **Location:** Outer Banks, North Carolina, USA
- **Built:** 1870
- **Height:** 63 meters

The tallest brick lighthouse in the United States, famous for its distinctive black-and-white spiral daymark. It was moved 880 meters inland in 1999 to protect it from coastal erosion.

### Peggy's Point Lighthouse

- **Location:** Peggy's Cove, Nova Scotia, Canada
- **Built:** 1915

One of the most photographed lighthouses in the world, perched on wave-worn granite.

## Anatomy of a lighthouse

- **Tower:** The vertical structure, built from stone, brick, cast iron, or reinforced concrete.
- **Lantern room:** The glass-enclosed space at the top that houses the light.
- **Lens:** Historically a Fresnel lens, which uses concentric prisms to focus light into a powerful beam.
- **Gallery:** The exterior walkway around the lantern room.
- **Keeper's quarters:** Living space for the lighthouse keeper, now typically automated or unused.

## Modern role

Most lighthouses today are automated, and many have been decommissioned as GPS and electronic navigation replaced visual aids. Preservation societies, museums, and heritage organizations now maintain many historic lighthouses as cultural landmarks and tourist attractions.
