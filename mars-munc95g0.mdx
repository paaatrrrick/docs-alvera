Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest planet in the solar system. Its reddish color, caused by iron oxide on its surface, earned it the nickname "the Red Planet."

## Key facts

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Distance from the Sun | About 228 million km (1.52 AU) |
| Diameter | About 6,779 km (roughly half of Earth's) |
| Length of day | About 24 hours 37 minutes |
| Length of year | About 687 Earth days |
| Surface gravity | About 38% of Earth's |
| Moons | 2 (Phobos and Deimos) |

## Surface and geology

Mars has the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, which rises about 22 km above the surrounding plains. The planet also hosts Valles Marineris, a canyon system more than 4,000 km long. Polar ice caps made of water ice and frozen carbon dioxide grow and shrink with the seasons.

## Atmosphere and climate

The Martian atmosphere is thin and made mostly of carbon dioxide. Surface pressure is less than 1% of Earth's. Temperatures average around -60 °C, and planet-wide dust storms can cover the surface for weeks.

## Water on Mars

Dried riverbeds, lake deposits, and minerals that form in water show that liquid water once flowed on Mars. Today, most water exists as ice in the polar caps and below the surface.

## Exploration

Many spacecraft have studied Mars, including:

- **Viking 1 and 2** — the first successful US landers, in 1976.
- **Sojourner, Spirit, and Opportunity** — early rovers that explored the surface.
- **Curiosity** — a rover studying Gale Crater since 2012.
- **Perseverance and Ingenuity** — a rover collecting samples and the first helicopter to fly on another planet, arriving in 2021.

Scientists continue to explore Mars to learn whether it ever supported life and to prepare for future human missions.
