Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest in the solar system, after Jupiter. It is a gas giant best known for its bright, wide ring system. This page covers its size, rings, atmosphere, moons, and the missions that have explored it.

## Size and orbit

Saturn is about 116,000 km across at its equator, roughly nine times the diameter of Earth. It takes about 29.5 Earth years to orbit the Sun, but it spins quickly: one day lasts only about 10.7 hours. Because it spins so fast, the planet bulges noticeably at the equator. Its average density is lower than that of water, which makes it the least dense planet in the solar system.

## Rings

Saturn's rings are made mostly of ice, with some rock and dust mixed in. The pieces range from tiny grains to chunks several meters wide. The three main rings are, from outermost to innermost, the A, B, and C rings. The Cassini Division, a gap about 4,800 km wide, separates the A ring from the B ring. The rings are hundreds of thousands of kilometers wide but in most places only tens of meters thick.

## Atmosphere

Saturn's atmosphere is mostly hydrogen and helium, with small amounts of methane, ammonia, and other compounds. Its pale yellow bands come from ammonia crystals in the upper atmosphere. A long-lasting six-sided jet stream, known as the hexagon, circles the north pole. Each side of the hexagon is longer than Earth's diameter.

## Moons

Saturn has more than 140 confirmed moons, more than any other planet. Two of them stand out:

- **Titan**: the largest moon of Saturn and the second largest in the solar system. It is the only moon with a thick atmosphere, which is mostly nitrogen. Its surface has lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane.
- **Enceladus**: a small, icy moon. Geysers near its south pole spray water vapor and ice into space from an ocean beneath its surface, which makes it a major target in the search for life.

## Exploration

- **Pioneer 11** made the first flyby of Saturn in 1979.
- **Voyager 1 and Voyager 2** flew past in 1980 and 1981 and sent back detailed images of the rings and moons.
- **Cassini-Huygens** orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017. Its Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005. The mission ended when Cassini made a planned plunge into Saturn's atmosphere.
