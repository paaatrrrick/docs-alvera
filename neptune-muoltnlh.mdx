Neptune is the eighth planet from the Sun and the farthest known planet in the solar system. It's an ice giant, like Uranus, with a deep blue colour and the strongest winds of any planet.

## Discovery

Neptune was the first planet found through mathematical prediction rather than by looking. Irregularities in Uranus's orbit led Urbain Le Verrier and John Couch Adams to calculate, each on their own, where an unseen planet should be. Johann Galle spotted Neptune on 23 September 1846, less than one degree from where Le Verrier predicted it would be.

## Size and orbit

- **Diameter:** about 49,500 km, almost four times the width of Earth.
- **Distance from the Sun:** about 30 AU (roughly 4.5 billion km).
- **Year:** one orbit around the Sun takes about 165 Earth years.
- **Day:** one rotation takes about 16 hours.

## Atmosphere and weather

Neptune's atmosphere is mostly hydrogen and helium, with small amounts of methane. The methane absorbs red light, which gives the planet its blue colour.

Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system, reaching more than 2,000 km/h. In 1989, Voyager 2 photographed the Great Dark Spot, a huge storm system about the size of Earth. It had disappeared by the time the Hubble Space Telescope looked in 1994, and new dark spots have formed since then.

## Moons and rings

Neptune has 16 known moons. The largest is Triton, which orbits in the opposite direction to Neptune's rotation (a retrograde orbit). Its orbit suggests Triton is a Kuiper Belt object that Neptune's gravity captured. Triton is also one of the few moons known to be geologically active, and Voyager 2 saw geysers of nitrogen gas erupting from its surface.

Neptune also has a system of faint, dark rings, made mostly of dust and ice particles.

## Exploration

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft that has visited Neptune. It flew past in August 1989 and sent back the first close-up images of the planet, its rings, and Triton. Since then, astronomers have studied Neptune with telescopes. In 2022, the James Webb Space Telescope captured the clearest view of Neptune's rings in decades.
