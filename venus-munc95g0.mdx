Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the closest planet to Earth. It is often called Earth's twin because the two planets are similar in size and mass, but conditions on Venus are very different.

## Quick facts

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Distance from the Sun | About 108 million km (0.72 AU) |
| Diameter | About 12,104 km |
| Length of a day (sidereal) | About 243 Earth days |
| Length of a year | About 225 Earth days |
| Moons | None |
| Average surface temperature | About 464 °C (867 °F) |

## Atmosphere

Venus has a thick atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide, with clouds of sulfuric acid. The surface pressure is about 92 times that of Earth. The dense atmosphere traps heat through a runaway greenhouse effect, which makes Venus the hottest planet in the solar system, hotter even than Mercury.

## Rotation

Venus rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction to most planets, a motion called retrograde rotation. As a result, the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east on Venus. A single day on Venus is longer than its year.

## Surface

The surface of Venus is dominated by volcanic plains, highland regions, and thousands of volcanoes. Large highland areas include Ishtar Terra and Aphrodite Terra. Scientists continue to study whether some volcanoes on Venus are still active.

## Exploration

Many spacecraft have visited Venus, including:

- **Venera program** (Soviet Union): the first landers to return images from the surface of another planet.
- **Magellan** (NASA): mapped nearly the entire surface using radar in the early 1990s.
- **Venus Express** (ESA): studied the atmosphere from orbit between 2006 and 2014.
- **Akatsuki** (JAXA): studied atmospheric dynamics from orbit starting in 2015.

## Visibility from Earth

After the Moon, Venus is the brightest natural object in the night sky. It is often visible shortly after sunset or before sunrise, which is why it is sometimes called the evening star or the morning star.
