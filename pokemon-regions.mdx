## Overview

Each main-series Pokémon generation introduces a new region for you to explore. A region defines the geography, culture, Gym Leaders, Elite Four, and the roster of Pokémon you can catch in the wild.

## Main-series regions

| Region | Generation | Games                           | Real-world inspiration |
| ------ | ---------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Kanto  | I          | Red, Blue, Yellow               | Kantō, Japan           |
| Johto  | II         | Gold, Silver, Crystal           | Kansai, Japan          |
| Hoenn  | III        | Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald         | Kyushu, Japan          |
| Sinnoh | IV         | Diamond, Pearl, Platinum        | Hokkaido, Japan        |
| Unova  | V          | Black, White                    | New York City, USA     |
| Kalos  | VI         | X, Y                            | France                 |
| Alola  | VII        | Sun, Moon                       | Hawaii, USA            |
| Galar  | VIII       | Sword, Shield                   | United Kingdom         |
| Paldea | IX         | Scarlet, Violet                 | Iberian Peninsula      |

## Legendary Pokémon by region

Every region is tied to at least one Legendary Pokémon that features on the box art of its main games.

- **Kanto** — Mewtwo, Mew, Articuno, Zapdos, Moltres
- **Johto** — Ho-Oh, Lugia, Raikou, Entei, Suicune
- **Hoenn** — Groudon, Kyogre, Rayquaza
- **Sinnoh** — Dialga, Palkia, Giratina, Arceus
- **Unova** — Reshiram, Zekrom, Kyurem
- **Kalos** — Xerneas, Yveltal, Zygarde
- **Alola** — Solgaleo, Lunala, Necrozma
- **Galar** — Zacian, Zamazenta, Eternatus
- **Paldea** — Koraidon, Miraidon

## Choosing where to start

If you're new to the series, Kanto (Red, Blue, Yellow, or the Let's Go remakes) is the most approachable entry point. For a modern open-world experience, start with Paldea in Scarlet or Violet.

For more on the franchise as a whole, see [Pokémon](/pokemon).
