Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in the solar system. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium, and its mass is more than twice that of all the other planets combined.

## Atmosphere

Jupiter's atmosphere is divided into bands of light zones and dark belts, driven by strong east-west winds. Its clouds are made of ammonia ice, ammonium hydrosulfide, and water. Jupiter has the shortest day of any planet, and its fast rotation causes it to bulge at the equator.

## The Great Red Spot

The Great Red Spot is a giant storm larger than Earth. It has been observed for at least 150 years, and possibly since the 1600s. Measurements show the storm has been slowly shrinking over time.

## Moons

Jupiter has dozens of moons. The four largest, known as the Galilean moons, were discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610:

- **Io**: the most volcanically active body in the solar system.
- **Europa**: has an icy crust that likely covers a subsurface ocean of liquid water.
- **Ganymede**: the largest moon in the solar system, bigger than the planet Mercury.
- **Callisto**: one of the most heavily cratered objects in the solar system.

## Rings and magnetic field

Jupiter has a faint ring system made mostly of dust, first discovered by Voyager 1 in 1979. The planet also has the strongest magnetic field of any planet in the solar system, which creates intense radiation belts and bright auroras near its poles.

## Exploration

Several spacecraft have studied Jupiter, including:

- **Pioneer 10 and 11** (NASA): the first spacecraft to fly past Jupiter in the 1970s.
- **Voyager 1 and 2** (NASA): returned detailed images of the planet, its rings, and its moons in 1979.
- **Galileo** (NASA): orbited Jupiter from 1995 to 2003 and dropped a probe into its atmosphere.
- **Juno** (NASA): has orbited Jupiter since 2016, studying its interior, atmosphere, and magnetic field.
- **JUICE** (ESA) and **Europa Clipper** (NASA): launched to study Jupiter's icy moons in detail.
