---
title: History of tea
---

Tea is one of the most popular drinks in the world, with a history that spans nearly 5,000 years. This page traces tea from its legendary discovery in ancient China, through its spread to Japan and Central Asia, to its arrival in Europe and its place in the modern world.

## Ancient origins in China

It is believed by many historians that tea was first discovered in China around 2737 BCE, when leaves from a wild tea tree were said to have been blown into a pot of boiling water that was being prepared by the emperor Shen Nong, and the resulting drink was found to be refreshing by him. Over the following centuries, tea was cultivated, processed, and consumed by monks, scholars, and nobles alike.

## Spread to Japan and beyond

Tea seeds were brought to Japan by Buddhist monks returning from China in the 9th century, and the practice of drinking powdered green tea was gradually developed by them into what would later be recognized and celebrated as the Japanese tea ceremony. From there, tea was carried along trade routes to Korea, Tibet, and Central Asia.

## Tea in Europe and the modern world

Tea was introduced to Europe by Dutch and Portuguese traders in the early 17th century, and it was quickly adopted by the British upper classes, who were so taken with it that large plantations were eventually established by the British East India Company in India and Ceylon to meet the ever-growing demand that had been created at home. Today, tea is enjoyed daily by billions of people in countless different styles.
