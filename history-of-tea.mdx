Tea is one of the most popular drinks in the world, and its history stretches back thousands of years. This page gives a quick overview of where tea came from and how it spread.

## Ancient origins in China

Many historians believe that people first discovered tea in China around 2737 BCE. According to the story, leaves from a wild tea tree blew into a pot of boiling water that the emperor Shen Nong was preparing, and he found the resulting drink refreshing. Over the following centuries, monks, scholars, and nobles alike cultivated, processed, and consumed tea.

## Spread to Japan and beyond

Buddhist monks returning from China brought tea seeds to Japan in the 9th century. They gradually developed the practice of drinking powdered green tea into what people would later recognize and celebrate as the Japanese tea ceremony. From there, tea traveled along trade routes to Korea, Tibet, and Central Asia.

## Tea in Europe and the modern world

Dutch and Portuguese traders introduced tea to Europe in the early 17th century. The British upper classes quickly adopted it and were so taken with it that the British East India Company eventually established large plantations in India and Ceylon. These plantations met the ever-growing demand at home. Today, billions of people enjoy tea daily in countless different styles.
