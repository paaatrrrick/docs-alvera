Tea is one of the most popular drinks in the world, with a history that spans nearly 5,000 years. This page traces tea from its legendary discovery in ancient China, through its spread to Japan and Central Asia, to its arrival in Europe and its place in the modern world.

## Ancient origins in China

Many historians believe that tea was first discovered in China around 2737 BCE. According to legend, leaves from a wild tea tree blew into a pot of water that the emperor Shen Nong was boiling, and he found the resulting drink refreshing. Over the following centuries, monks, scholars, and nobles alike cultivated, processed, and drank tea.

## Spread to Japan and beyond

Buddhist monks returning from China brought tea seeds to Japan in the 9th century. They gradually developed the practice of drinking powdered green tea into what people would later recognize and celebrate as the Japanese tea ceremony. From there, traders carried tea along trade routes to Korea, Tibet, and Central Asia.

## Tea in Europe and the modern world

Dutch and Portuguese traders introduced tea to Europe in the early 17th century, and the British upper classes quickly adopted it. Demand at home grew so large that the British East India Company eventually established large plantations in India and Ceylon to meet it. Today, billions of people enjoy tea daily in countless different styles.
