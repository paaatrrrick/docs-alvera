# Lightsabers

A lightsaber is an energy weapon built around a kyber crystal. Jedi, Sith, and other Force-sensitive warriors use lightsabers for defense, ceremony, and combat.

## Anatomy

Most lightsabers combine three main elements:

- **Hilt**: The handle contains the emitter, power cell, controls, and focusing systems.
- **Kyber crystal**: The Force-attuned crystal focuses energy into a stable blade.
- **Blade**: The plasma-like energy blade is contained by a magnetic field and can cut through many materials.

Designs vary widely. A single-bladed saber is the most common. Double-bladed, crossguard, curved-hilt, and shoto designs support different fighting styles.

## Blade colors

The crystal's bond with its wielder influences the blade's color. Blue and green blades are strongly associated with Jedi. Red blades are associated with Sith and other dark-side users. Other colors appear across the galaxy and do not, by themselves, define a user's allegiance.

A dark-side wielder can corrupt a kyber crystal in a process often called **bleeding**, which produces a red blade. A Force user can later heal a corrupted crystal, producing a white blade.

## Combat forms

Lightsaber combat developed into several traditional forms:

| Form | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| I: Shii-Cho | Foundational attacks and disarming techniques |
| II: Makashi | Precision and lightsaber-to-lightsaber dueling |
| III: Soresu | Defensive movement and endurance |
| IV: Ataru | Acrobatic attacks supported by the Force |
| V: Shien and Djem So | Counterattacks and strength |
| VI: Niman | A balanced style that combines several disciplines |
| VII: Juyo and Vaapad | Aggressive, demanding techniques |

A warrior's form reflects their training, physique, temperament, and battlefield needs. Experienced users often combine techniques rather than follow one form exclusively.

## Safety and discipline

A lightsaber is dangerous even to its wielder. Training emphasizes controlled movement, awareness of surroundings, and restraint. Jedi traditionally treat the weapon as a responsibility rather than a status symbol.
