## Overview

A burger is a sandwich made of a cooked patty placed inside a sliced bun. The patty is traditionally ground beef, but you can also find burgers made with chicken, turkey, fish, or plant-based proteins. Burgers became popular in the United States in the early 20th century and are now eaten around the world.

## Components

Most burgers share the same basic building blocks.

| Component  | Common options                                        |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Bun        | Sesame seed, brioche, potato, pretzel, lettuce wrap   |
| Patty      | Beef, chicken, turkey, salmon, black bean, plant-based |
| Cheese     | American, cheddar, Swiss, pepper jack, blue           |
| Toppings   | Lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles, bacon, avocado       |
| Sauces     | Ketchup, mustard, mayonnaise, barbecue, special sauce |

## Popular styles

| Style         | Description                                                        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cheeseburger  | A classic burger topped with melted cheese                         |
| Smash burger  | Thin patties pressed hard onto a hot griddle for a crispy crust    |
| Slider        | A small burger, often served in groups of two or three             |
| Jucy Lucy     | A patty with cheese sealed inside, originating in Minneapolis      |
| Veggie burger | A meat-free patty made from vegetables, grains, or legumes         |
| Butter burger | A patty cooked or served with butter, popular in Wisconsin         |

## Cooking methods

You can cook a burger patty several ways. Each method affects flavor and texture.

- **Grilling**: Cooks over an open flame and adds a smoky, charred flavor.
- **Griddling**: Cooks on a flat-top surface and builds an even, browned crust.
- **Pan-frying**: Cooks in a skillet and works well indoors.
- **Broiling**: Cooks under direct overhead heat in an oven.

<Tip>
  Cook ground beef to an internal temperature of 160°F (71°C), as recommended by the USDA.
</Tip>

## Build a basic burger

<Steps>
  <Step title="Form the patty">
    Shape ground beef into a patty slightly wider than your bun. Press a shallow dimple into the center so the patty stays flat while it cooks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Season">
    Season both sides with salt and pepper just before cooking.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cook">
    Cook the patty over medium-high heat for 3–4 minutes per side. Add cheese during the last minute so it melts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Toast the bun">
    Toast the cut sides of the bun on the grill or pan until golden.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assemble">
    Place the patty on the bottom bun, add your toppings and sauces, and close with the top bun.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Regional variations

- **United States**: Home to countless styles, from California's fresh-topped burgers to Oklahoma's onion-smashed burgers.
- **Australia**: Often topped with beetroot and a fried egg.
- **Japan**: Features the teriyaki burger and rice buns.
- **Mexico**: Hamburguesas may include ham, sausage, pineapple, and jalapeños.
