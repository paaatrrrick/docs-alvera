Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system and the closest to the Sun. This page covers its size, orbit, surface, internal structure, and the spacecraft that have explored it.

## Size and orbit

Mercury is about 4,880 km across, only slightly larger than Earth's Moon. It orbits the Sun once every 88 Earth days, which is the shortest year of any planet.

Mercury is in a 3:2 spin-orbit resonance: it rotates three times on its axis for every two orbits around the Sun. Because of this, a full solar day on Mercury (from one sunrise to the next) lasts about 176 Earth days.

## Surface and atmosphere

Mercury's surface is heavily cratered and looks a lot like the Moon's. Its largest known impact feature is the Caloris Basin, which is about 1,550 km wide.

Mercury has no real atmosphere. It has only a very thin exosphere of atoms knocked off the surface by the solar wind and micrometeorite impacts. With no atmosphere to hold in heat, temperatures swing from about 430°C during the day to about -180°C at night.

## Internal structure

Mercury has an unusually large iron core that makes up about 85% of the planet's radius. This makes it the second densest planet, after Earth. Mercury also has a global magnetic field, though it is only about 1% as strong as Earth's.

## Exploration

- **Mariner 10** (NASA) flew past Mercury three times in 1974 and 1975 and mapped about 45% of its surface.
- **MESSENGER** (NASA) orbited Mercury from 2011 to 2015. It mapped the whole planet and found evidence of water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters.
- **BepiColombo** (ESA and JAXA) launched in 2018 and is scheduled to enter orbit around Mercury in the mid-2020s.
