---
title: Tea history overview
description: Trace tea from Shen Nong's legendary discovery in ancient China through Buddhist monks in Japan and Korea to Dutch traders and English afternoon tea.
---

People have enjoyed tea for thousands of years. Its story spans dynasties, continents, and cultures, shaped by farmers, monks, merchants, and monarchs who each left a mark on how the leaf is grown, brewed, and shared.

## Ancient origins in China

Legend credits the emperor Shen Nong with discovering tea around 2737 BCE, when the wind blew leaves into a pot of boiling water his servants had prepared. Early drinkers took tea mainly as a medicine. Only much later did the wider population adopt it as an everyday beverage.

## Spread to Japan and Korea

Buddhist monks studying in China carried tea seeds back to Japan. The tea ceremony that later developed there drew heavily on the Zen practices those monks had learned. Korea received tea through similar religious and trade connections.

## Arrival in Europe

Dutch and Portuguese traders brought tea to Europe in the early 1600s. Catherine of Braganza popularized it in England, where the wealthy classes quickly took it up as fashionable. By the 1800s, tea had become a staple of daily life, and afternoon tea grew into a beloved tradition.
