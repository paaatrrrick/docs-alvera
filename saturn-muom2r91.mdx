Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in the solar system, after Jupiter. It's a gas giant best known for its rings, which are the brightest and most extensive of any planet.

## Composition and structure

Saturn is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Its average density is lower than that of water, making it the least dense planet in the solar system. Saturn also spins fast: one day lasts only about 10.7 hours. That rapid rotation flattens the planet, so it bulges noticeably at the equator and looks flattened at the poles.

## The rings

Saturn's rings are made mostly of water ice particles. The particles range in size from tiny grains to chunks as large as a house. The main rings are named A, B, and C. The widest gap between them is the Cassini Division, which separates the A and B rings. The rings stretch hundreds of thousands of kilometers across, but in most places the ring plane is only about 10 meters to 1 kilometer thick.

## Moons

Saturn has well over 100 confirmed moons. Two stand out:

- **Titan** is Saturn's largest moon and the only moon in the solar system with a thick atmosphere. Its surface has lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane.
- **Enceladus** is a small, icy moon that shoots geysers of water ice from its south pole. The geysers are fed by an ocean beneath its surface.

## Atmosphere and weather

Saturn's atmosphere has bands of cloud, similar to Jupiter's but more muted. At the north pole, a long-lasting, six-sided jet stream called the hexagon circles the planet. Winds in the upper atmosphere can reach about 1,800 km/h.

## Exploration

- **Pioneer 11** made the first flyby of Saturn in 1979.
- **Voyager 1 and Voyager 2** flew past in 1980 and 1981 and returned detailed images of the rings and moons.
- **Cassini–Huygens** orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017. Its Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005. Cassini ended its mission by diving into Saturn's atmosphere.
- **Dragonfly** is an upcoming NASA mission that will send a rotorcraft to explore Titan's surface.
